Final Project (6 marks) Machine Learning 2026 – LIACD, LECD, LEEC  Informatics Engineering Department     1. Problem Statement In this assignment, you must apply the methods you have learned throughout the semester to a stress level classification problem, whose data set is available for download on the Kaggle platform at the following link: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/jayjoshi37/sleep-screen-time-and-stress-analysis  The goal of this project is to tackle three problems: A. Predict the stress level in a binary setting. B. Predict the stress level in a 5-class setting. C. Predict the stress level in a 10-class setting, from no or low to maximum stress.  2. Dataset Description (adapted from Kaggle) As mobile devices become inseparable from daily life, understanding their impact on mental well-being is critical. The ‘Sleep, Screen Time and Stress’ data set explores the relationship between smartphone usage, sleep patterns, and stress levels, featuring 15,000 synthetic records detailing the lifestyles and mobile habits of a diverse population. By tracking metrics such as pre-sleep screen time, physical activity levels, and caffeine intake, this data can be modeled to classify stress.  3. Objectives This project is divided into the following three modelling scenarios: 3.1. Scenario A (Binary Problem) – The objective of this scenario is to classify each instance as, for example, ‘no stress’ (0) or ‘stress’ (1); 3.2. Scenario B (Multi-Class Problem 1) – The objective of this scenario is to classify the level of stress as one of five possible levels, e.g. , ‘no stress’ (0), ‘low’ (1), ‘moderate’ (2), ‘high’ (3) and ‘extreme’ (4); 3.3. Scenario C (Multi-Class Problem 2) – The objective of this scenario is to classify the level of stress as one of 10 possible, e.g., from ‘no stress’ (0) to ‘maximum’ (9).  Two important notes: 1. The stress level is originally recorded as a continuous scale. Make the necessary arrangements to transform the associated variable to match each scenarios' requirements. For example, in the binary case, you can set the ‘no stress’/‘low stress’ class to encompass all the values in [0, 5[, whereas the ‘stress’ will contain all within [5, 10] (equal-width binning).  Other discretization methods can be used, such as equal-frequency binning or K-means clustering to identify natural groupings, depending on the variable’s distribution.  2. The class nature and names are flexible. You can change them to reflect your own preference.
